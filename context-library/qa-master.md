## Common Questions

### "Why are you leaving your current role?"
Context (private): laid off from Keystone.ai. Position as seeking larger scale. Never deny the layoff if asked directly.

**Primary script:** "I spent the last two-plus years at Keystone.ai taking an agentic AI platform from 0→1, helping drive the company's shift from consulting to product, reaching $10M ARR and deploying with five enterprises. I'm proud of that. What I want next is to apply what I've learned about making agents reliable in production (evaluation, human-in-the-loop design, risk-tiered autonomy) at much larger scale, on a product where agent reliability is the core problem."

**If asked directly ("Are you still there?" / "Were you laid off?"):** "My role was part of a restructuring at Keystone.ai. It gave me the push to go after what I'd been wanting anyway: enterprise AI at a much larger scale." Keep it to one sentence, then move back to what you built.

### "Why do you want to work at [company]?"
Template: (1) their specific agent/AI platform bet and where it's going; (2) the connection to your work: production agents (forecasting agent), ML platforms (Amazon MLOps), or your domains (supply chain, fintech); (3) the scale or customer base you can't reach at Keystone. `/interview-prep` customizes this per company.

### "What is your greatest weakness?"
[CONFIRM — must be real, ideally from performance feedback]
Candidate prompts to help you pick (not claims about you):
- Holding onto technical detail too long before delegating, as a hands-on builder
- Over-investing in completeness before sharing early drafts with executives
- Pushing too hard for platform reusability when a client needed a quick custom fix
Pick one that's true, add the feedback you got, and say what you now do differently.

### "What is your biggest failure?"
[CONFIRM — needs a real work failure]
Partial material available: the job-search agent that declared success without searching comprehensively (Story 9 in the experience library) is a good learning story, but it's a side project. Interviewers usually want a work failure. Candidates to explore: a Keystone client deployment that stalled, an Amazon launch that missed its target, or an MLOps adoption push that didn't land.
